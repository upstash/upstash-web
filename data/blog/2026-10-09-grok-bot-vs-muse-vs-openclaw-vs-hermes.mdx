Two months ago "personal AI assistant" mostly meant OpenClaw running on someone's Mac mini. Then Grok Bot shipped in August and Meta Muse in September, and Hermes grew into a serious open-source alternative to OpenClaw. So the obvious question now is which one to use.

The four split into two pairs. Grok Bot and Muse are hosted products you sign up for: the vendor runs the computer, decides which models you can use and keeps your data. OpenClaw and Hermes are open-source projects you run yourself: you pick the model and keep the data, and you also keep the machine running. Same problem, very different trade-offs.

<Summary>
  - Hosted agents (Grok Bot, Muse) are the easiest start. You don't run anything, but you don't pick the model or where your data lives either.
  - Open-source agents (OpenClaw, Hermes) give you the model choice and the data. You also get the upkeep: a machine, updates, and security.
  - MCP support is now normal. The difference is who can add a server: you, or only the vendor.
  - Pick by the job. Errands, computer tasks and "I want to own it" point to different agents.
</Summary>

## The short version

| Agent | Open source | MCP | Where it runs | Entry price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grok Bot | No | Yes, plugins and custom servers | Hosted cloud computer | Included with paid Cursor or SuperGrok plans |
| Meta Muse | No | Through Meta's reviewed connector directory | Hosted, US and Canada | Free tier, then $20 or $100 a month |
| OpenClaw | Yes, MIT | Yes, native | Your own machine or server | Free, you pay for hosting and the model |
| Hermes Agent | Yes, MIT | Yes, native | Your own machine or server | Free, you pay for hosting and the model |

Prices and availability are as of early October 2026. All of these change fast.

## Hosted agents

### Grok Bot

[Grok Bot](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview) from SpaceXAI gives you named Bots that each work on a persistent cloud computer with a browser, a filesystem and a terminal. You message them from the desktop or mobile app, and they keep working when your laptop is closed.

What I like:

- **Several Bots, one team.** They run in parallel, message each other and hand tasks over.
- **Skills from demonstration.** You walk a Bot through a workflow once and it can save it as a skill and run it on a schedule.
- **Easy MCP.** Connectors install as plugins from the Marketplace, and you can add custom MCP servers too.

What to know:

- **Hosted only.** It runs only on Cursor-hosted cloud computers. No self-hosting, no running it inside your own network.
- **One computer per user.** All your Bots share the same files, browser sessions and logins. Separate Bots are not separate security boundaries.
- **No model picker.** The model is managed for you.

Pricing: it's included with every paid individual Cursor plan and Cursor Teams, or with a linked SuperGrok subscription. Usage resets weekly.

Through MCP it can also drive machines outside its own, for example [an Upstash Box used as a second remote computer](https://upstash.com/blog/i-gave-grok-bot-a-remote-computer).

### Meta Muse

[Muse](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) is Meta's consumer agent. It runs on a dedicated "Muse Secure VM" with its own browser, and you talk to it in the Muse app or in WhatsApp.

What I like:

- **No learning curve.** It's built for errands: email, bookings, shopping, plans.
- **A thoughtful safety design.** A separate Sentinel agent has to approve anything Muse sends to the internet. Muse can't see your passwords or payment methods, and checkout goes through one-time cards.
- **Free to start.** Most everyday use is free.

What to know:

- **Limited regions.** It launched in the US and has since reached Canada. No other countries yet.
- **Locked to Meta's model.** It runs on Muse Spark.
- **Training is opt-out.** You can opt out of your interactions being used to train Meta's models.
- **MCP is for developers, not users.** Meta's connector platform accepts MCP servers, but they go through Meta's review before they show up in the directory.

Pricing: free with a weekly limit, then [Power at $20 a month or Maximum at $100 a month](https://www.meta.com/help/subscriptions/1021145227643680/).

## Open-source agents

### OpenClaw

[OpenClaw](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw) is the one that started this whole wave. It's an open-source personal assistant that runs on your own machine and talks to you through the chat apps you already use: WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, iMessage, Signal, Teams and more. One Gateway process connects your model, tools and channels.

It's MIT licensed and sits at over 390k GitHub stars.

What I like:

- **Every channel.** If you want an assistant in your existing chats, nothing else covers as many.
- **Any model.** You bring your provider.
- **Huge ecosystem** of skills and community guides.
- **Native MCP.** You register MCP servers in its config or with the `openclaw mcp` CLI.

What to know:

- **You run it.** The daemon, updates and channel setup are on you.
- **Tools run on the host by default**, unless you configure sandboxing.
- **Be careful with skills.** Security researchers found hundreds of [malicious skills on ClawHub](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-openclaw-clawhub-supply-chain-risk-2026070/) this year. Read what you install.

Where it runs: macOS, Linux or Windows, so a laptop, a Mac mini, a VPS, or a cloud sandbox like Upstash Box ([setup guide](https://upstash.com/docs/box/guides/openclaw-setup)). Since tools run on the host, a machine that isn't your personal one is the safer default.

### Hermes Agent

[Hermes Agent](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent) from Nous Research is the closest open-source alternative to OpenClaw. It has a terminal UI and a messaging gateway (WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, Signal, email and more), its own cron scheduler, memory across sessions, and a learning loop that turns repeated work into skills.

It's MIT licensed with over 250k GitHub stars.

What I like:

- **Any model, easy to switch.** `hermes model` and you're on another provider.
- **Runs on many backends.** Local, Docker, SSH, Modal, Daytona and more.
- **MCP both ways.** It connects to MCP servers, and it can also act as one with `hermes mcp serve`.
- **Cron with delivery.** "Send me a summary every morning" lands in your chat.

What to know:

- **You own the config, secrets and updates.**
- **The install is big.** It wants a real machine with some memory.
- **Smaller ecosystem** than OpenClaw, though it's growing fast.

Where it runs: anything from a $5 VPS to a GPU box, plus serverless backends like Modal and Daytona that sleep when idle. It also runs on a cloud sandbox like Upstash Box ([setup guide](https://upstash.com/docs/box/guides/hermes-setup), and a [WhatsApp walkthrough](https://upstash.com/blog/personal-assistant-in-messaging-apps-hermes-upstash-box)).

## Where do the open-source ones run?

This is the question the hosted agents answer for you, and the open-source ones leave to you. A messaging assistant is only useful if it answers, so whatever you pick has to stay on.

- **Your laptop.** Fine for trying it out. It stops when the lid closes, and the agent runs next to your own files and logins.
- **A Mac mini or home server.** Always on and cheap once you own it, and handy if you want the agent close to your own devices. You maintain the hardware and the network.
- **A VPS.** Always on and reachable from anywhere. You take on the server: updates, disk, a process manager, firewall rules.
- **A cloud sandbox.** An isolated Linux machine you don't have to administer, such as Upstash Box, Modal or Daytona. Some sleep when idle, which suits scheduled jobs. For a chat gateway you want one that stays up, like a [keep-alive Box](https://upstash.com/docs/box/overall/keep-alive) with an init script that restarts the gateway.

Whichever you choose, the point is the same: give the agent its own computer instead of yours, and keep its config, memory and sessions somewhere you control.

## How I'd pick

- **Personal errands, no setup, you're in the US or Canada:** Muse.
- **Computer tasks, several agents working together, you already pay for Cursor or SuperGrok:** Grok Bot.
- **You want to pick the model and keep the data:** OpenClaw for the most channels and the biggest ecosystem, Hermes for a leaner setup with cron and MCP built in.

Whichever you choose, start with read-only tasks and give it more access as you trust it. Every one of these can send, buy or delete if you let it.

You may not pick just one, either. If you end up using Grok Bot and Muse side by side, each one wants its own access to your Gmail, Calendar or Slack. A layer like [Upsy](https://upsy.ai/home) sits between your assistants and your apps: you connect the apps once, then choose per assistant and per app whether it gets full access, read-only, specific actions, or nothing. It keeps a history of what each assistant did and alerts you on mass deletes, sends or reads.
