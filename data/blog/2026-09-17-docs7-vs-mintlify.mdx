Documentation should get you to an answer quickly. While we built [Docs7](https://context7.com/docs7), we wanted to know how its pages perform against Mintlify on real documentation, with screenshots, video, and custom components. A fast text-only page would not tell us much.

We hosted 17 pages from the Upstash and Context7 docs on both platforms and tested them with Google PageSpeed Insights and DebugBear. Both hosts got the same page paths and the same test settings. Google tested each URL 10 times per device, and we report the median, not the best run.

## The results

**Docs7 won 33 of 34 Google comparisons and tied one.** Its average score was 80.1 vs. 58.9 on mobile and 94.9 vs. 88.4 on desktop. DebugBear favored Docs7 on all 10 page pairs it tested, on both devices.

![Average scores and page wins for Google and DebugBear, on mobile and desktop. Docs7 is orange; Mintlify is green.](/blog/docs7-vs-mintlify/typical-results.svg)

The gap is widest on mobile. The expandable API fields page scored 85 vs. 46.5, and the long CLI guide scored 89.5 vs. 54. The gap is narrowest on desktop, where Mintlify tied the release notes page at 96.5 and came within one point on two others.

Docs7 has slow pages too. The long API reference scored 57 on mobile. That is ahead of Mintlify's 46.5, but it is the lowest Docs7 score in the set.

## What we tested

We used two documentation projects, each hosted on both platforms:

- **Upstash:** [Docs7](https://upstash-docs.docs7.io/docs/introduction) and [Mintlify](https://upstash.mintlify.dev/docs/introduction).
- **Context7:** [Docs7](https://upstash-context7.docs7.io/docs/overview) and [Mintlify](https://context7.mintlify.dev/docs/overview).

We picked pages that stress different parts of a documentation site:

- Images and video test download cost and layout stability.
- Long API, SDK, and CLI references test large pages and code blocks.
- Tabs, steps, accordions, diagrams, a file tree, and a custom React component add rendering and JavaScript work.

The [full results on GitHub](https://github.com/upstash/upstash-web/blob/101b417e46df9239e99bd24b0da68ee107594188/benchmarks/docs7-vs-mintlify.md) list all 17 pairs, with links to both the Docs7 and Mintlify URLs in every row.

## Results by page type

Each dot is the average of the page medians in that group, and the line between the dots is the gap. Scores are out of 100. Docs7 led in every group on both devices.

![Google scores by page type on mobile and desktop, using 10 runs per URL. Docs7 is orange; Mintlify is green.](/blog/docs7-vs-mintlify/google-by-page-type.svg)

On mobile, the largest gaps were on diagrams (88.5 vs. 59.5) and long guides (91 vs. 63.4). Pages with video were the slowest group for Docs7, at 64.5. On desktop, both hosts scored above 90 in most groups, and images made the largest difference, 98 vs. 79.5.

## Why Docs7 loads faster

The measurements point to work in the browser. The Docs7 build prepares responsive image sizes, sets image dimensions so the layout does not shift, and defers images below the first screen. Docs7 pages also blocked the browser for less time. Across the 17 pages, the median blocking time was 199 ms vs. 389 ms on mobile and 166 ms vs. 228 ms on desktop.

Docs7 also compiles documentation into static HTML at build time, and Cloudflare's [static-asset network](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/) serves those files. The browser receives the content directly, with JavaScript for interactive features.

The video and component catalog page shows the effect. On desktop, Docs7 showed the largest content sooner and blocked the browser for less time:

![Video-page desktop medians. Docs7 shows the largest content at 791 ms vs. 1,102 ms, with 493 ms vs. 682 ms of blocking time.](/blog/docs7-vs-mintlify/google-workflow-comparison.svg)

Mintlify painted the first content earlier here, 524 ms vs. 565 ms. On mobile, the median largest-content paint was 4.20 seconds on Docs7 vs. 12.91 seconds on Mintlify. Mobile runs on this page varied a lot on both hosts, from 2.9 to 16.5 seconds on Docs7 and from 3.3 to 21.1 seconds on Mintlify.

We do not know how Mintlify works internally, and these tests do not show how much of the gap comes from each design choice.

## Method and limits

Google ran all 680 tests on September 17, 2026, using Lighthouse 13.4.1. DebugBear results include follow-up runs on September 18. For repeated tests we use the median on both hosts, following [Google's guidance on repeated runs](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/lighthouse/blob/main/docs/variability.md#run-lighthouse-multiple-times). Every page has equal weight, and we keep devices and services separate.

These are lab tests of the first page load on deployed sites. We did not play the videos or measure interaction latency. We did not control cache state, and the paired runs were not always simultaneous.

We also ran Pingdom load-time checks from San Francisco, Frankfurt, and Tokyo. Most pages have only one run per URL, which is too thin for a regional speed claim, so those numbers are [in the appendix](https://github.com/upstash/upstash-web/blob/101b417e46df9239e99bd24b0da68ee107594188/benchmarks/docs7-vs-mintlify.md#pingdom-exploratory-load-time-checks) only.
